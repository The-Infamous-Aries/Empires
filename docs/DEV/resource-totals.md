### Balance Assessment

**Bronze — Intentionally concentrated in buildings:**
- Zero military unit usage (soldiers, tanks, aircraft, ships, spies, missiles, nukes all use no bronze)
- Appears in every wonder (15,700 qty) and 14 of 30 projects (3,275 qty)
- **Balance: Intentional.** Bronze is the "building-only" resource, compensating for its complete absence from military costs. This forces nations to choose between military expansion and infrastructure investment.

**High-Tier Military Resource Dominance (Late Game):**
- **Nukes** consume 100 each of aluminum, gasoline, uranium per unit — at just 100 nukes: 10,000 aluminum, 10,000 gasoline, 10,000 uranium. This **exceeds** all project+wonder totals for each resource (aluminum 10,500, gasoline 8,775, uranium 8,500).
- **Missiles** consume 25 each of aluminum, gasoline, lead per unit — at 100 missiles: 2,500 aluminum (~24% of project+wonder aluminum), 2,500 gasoline (~28%), 2,500 lead (~32%).
- **Balance: Intentional late-game sink.** Military becomes the primary resource consumer at scale, while projects/wonders are one-time infrastructure investments. This creates a meaningful late-game resource drain.

**Low-Tier Military Resource Usage:**
- Tanks (steel 5, gasoline 1, lead 1), Ships (steel 10, gasoline 3, lead 3), Aircraft (aluminum 5, gasoline 2, lead 2) — small per-unit costs
- At 100 tanks: only 500 steel (~4.5% of project+wonder steel), 100 gasoline (~1.1%), 100 lead (~1.3%)
- **Balance: Well-scaled.** Conventional military has modest resource costs; nuclear/missile forces drive late-game consumption.

**Resource Distribution Evenness:**
- No resource is excluded from any category except bronze (zero military, by design)
- Wonders consume 83-90% of every resource (projects are smaller one-time builds)
- Projects consume 8-17% of every resource
- Military per-build-set consumes 0-1.5% of every resource individually, but scales with army size

**Project vs Wonder Disparity:**
- Wonders cost 5.2x more resources than projects combined (63,600 vs 12,300 qty)
- Wonders cost 5.7x more money than projects ($2,560M vs $452M)
- 27 wonders vs 30 projects — similar count, but wonders are individually much more expensive
- **Balance: Intentional.** Wonders are end-game, nation-defining buildings; projects are mid-game enhancements.

**All-7-Resource Buildings (Max Complexity):**
- Broadcasting (50 each), foodProduction (50-200 each), waterProduction (50-200 each) — projects requiring all 7
- supremeAssembly, cleanAssembly, internet, abundanceTemple, aqueductOfLife (750 each) — wonders requiring all 7
- supremeMining, cleanMining (300 each) — wonders requiring all 7
- **Balance: These are the "completionist" buildings** — require a fully developed resource economy across all 7 minerals, encouraging trade or World Bank usage if a nation lacks domestic production.

### Balance Impact Analysis

**Bronze Becomes the Dominant Bottleneck:**
- Bronze now represents **40%** of all building resources (37,950/94,875), up from ~25% pre-doubling
- Bronze exceeds steel by 3.4x, aluminum by 3.6x, copper by 3.7x, uranium by 4.5x, lead by 4.9x, gasoline by 4.3x
- Every wonder requires bronze (31,400 qty across wonders alone), plus 14 of 30 projects (6,550 qty)

**Strategic Implications:**
1. **Bronze becomes mandatory for all building** — A nation cannot build any wonder without bronze, and most projects require it. This makes bronze production (bronze smelter → bronze casting) a gatekeeper for all infrastructure.
2. **Zero military usage remains** — Bronze is still useless for soldiers, tanks, aircraft, ships, spies, missiles, nukes. Nations must choose: grind bronze for buildings, or skip bronze and focus purely on military.
3. **Trade/World Bank dependency increases** — Nations without domestic bronze production must trade heavily or buy from World Bank to build anything. This strengthens the resource economy loop.
4. **Wonder construction becomes significantly harder** — Wonders already cost 5.2x more than projects; doubling bronze makes them even more expensive (bronze alone would be 31,400 qty across all wonders).
5. **Project count drops effectively** — 14 of 30 projects require bronze; doubling means those projects cost substantially more, potentially making some (like commandCenter at bronze 125→250→500, missileSilo at bronze 150→300) prohibitively expensive early game.

**Bronze-Heavy Wonders (After Doubling):**
- governmentMonument: bronze 2,000
- religiousMonument: bronze 2,000
- enhancedSalvage: bronze 2,000
- enhancedNuclearWarheads: bronze 2,000
- supremeRecycling: bronze 2,000
- supremeAssembly: bronze 2,000
- cleanAssembly: bronze 2,000
- internet: bronze 1,000
- abundanceTemple: bronze 1,500
- aqueductOfLife: bronze 1,500

**Verdict: High-impact change.** Doubling bronze makes it the single most important building resource in the game, creating a strong economic gatekeeper. Nations without bronze production would be severely limited in building wonders and many projects, while military-focused nations could ignore bronze entirely. In a 10,000-nation world, this would create a vibrant bronze trading market — war-focused nations overproduce other resources and underproduce bronze, while building-focused nations need bronze but may not produce it. The World Bank becomes the critical price discovery mechanism for bronze.
